Concept A
Data Preprocessing & Cleaning

Legal documents contain formatting irregularities, line breaks, and citations. The preprocessing module:

  • Missing values: Drops rows lacking text content or category labels.
  • Duplicate detection: Identifies and prunes duplicate records based on document text hashing to prevent test contamination.
  • Domain-aware stopword removal: Standard stopwords (like "the", "an") are removed, but critical legal negations and modal verbs (not, shall, may, liable, covenant) are strictly preserved.
Concept B
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Systematic exploratory analysis verifies corpus health before model training:

  • Document length metrics: Tracks character and word token distributions (mean, median, standard deviation).
  • Class imbalance analysis: Audits sample representation per legal class to prevent majority-class bias.
  • Vocabulary profiling: Analyzes term frequency to determine optimal min_df and max_df thresholds.
Concept C
Feature Engineering: TF-IDF & N-Grams

Converts unstructured legal text into sparse numerical vector matrices:

$$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \log\left(\frac{1 + |D|}{1 + |\{d \in D : t \in d\}|}\right) + 1$$
  • Unigrams & Bigrams: Captures compound legal phrases such as "receiving party", "confidential information", and "gross misconduct".
  • Sublinear TF Scaling: Replaces term frequency with $1 + \log(\text{TF})$ to prevent frequent mentions of a term from dominating.
Concept D
Supervised Classification Algorithms

The system benchmarks three diverse statistical learning algorithms:

  • Multinomial Naive Bayes: Generative probabilistic model applying Bayes' theorem with feature conditional independence assumptions. Fast and effective for text.
  • Logistic Regression: Discriminative linear classifier modeling posterior probabilities via the softmax function with L2 ridge regularization.
  • Linear SVM (Support Vector Machine): Maximizes geometric margin between hyperplane boundaries, highly resilient in high-dimensional sparse text vectors.
Concept E & F
Validation & Performance Metrics

Rigorous validation protocols prevent over-optimistic reporting:

  • Stratified Train/Test Split: Preserves identical percentage of each legal category in both training and test partitions.
  • Macro F1-Score: Primary selection metric. Computes harmonic mean of Precision and Recall per class, then averages across classes unweighted.
  • Confusion Matrix: Explicitly reveals cross-category misclassifications (e.g. Service Agreements confused with NDAs).
Concept G, J & K
Tuning, Attribution & Persistence

Enterprise machine learning best practices:

  • GridSearchCV: Exhaustively explores regularization strength $C$, smoothing $\alpha$, and n-gram boundaries over internal cross-validation folds.
  • Feature Attribution: Ranks model coefficients $w_i$ for linear classifiers or log-likelihood ratios for Naive Bayes to highlight words driving predictions.
  • Pipeline Persistence: Entire scikit-learn Pipeline containing the fitted vectorizer and classifier is serialized via joblib.

Viva Examination Questions & Model Answers

Simple Bag-of-Words only counts raw frequencies. In legal documents, boilerplate words such as "party", "agreement", and "clause" appear repeatedly across all categories and would overshadow discriminative terms. TF-IDF downweights terms appearing across the whole corpus via the Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) factor, while boosting category-defining terms like "sublease" (Lease) or "indemnification" (Service Agreement).

Accuracy is misleading when class distributions are imbalanced. A classifier could achieve 90% accuracy simply by predicting majority classes while failing completely on rare contract types. Macro F1-Score computes the F1-score independently for each class and calculates the unweighted mean, ensuring every legal category is treated with equal importance regardless of sample count.

By wrapping the TfidfVectorizer and classifier inside a sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline and splitting the raw text into Train and Test partitions before feature extraction. The vectorizer calls fit_transform() only on the training set. The test set is processed strictly with transform(), meaning terms present solely in the test set never leak into the vocabulary.

Standard LinearSVC optimizes hinge loss and only computes signed distances to the separating hyperplane (decision scores), which lack probabilistic interpretation and cannot be directly summed to 1.0. CalibratedClassifierCV fits a sigmoid logistic curve (Platt Scaling) over the decision function outputs to produce mathematically valid posterior probability estimates.

Supervised classification trains models using known ground truth category labels ($y$) to predict discrete target classes. K-Means is an unsupervised clustering algorithm that ignores labels entirely and groups documents into $k$ clusters solely by minimizing the Euclidean distance between document TF-IDF vectors and cluster centroids. Discovered clusters indicate statistical lexical patterns, not legal validity.
